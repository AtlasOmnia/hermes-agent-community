# GPT-6 Astra after heavy use in Hermes: I spend less time explaining what I mean

**Posted by u/LittleTreeZZZZZ on 2026-09-07 · 14 points (88% upvoted) · 18 comments**

I posted earlier asking how people were finding GPT-6 Astra. There weren't any replies at the time, so I thought I'd come back with my own experience after using it quite a bit.
I've nearly used up my weekly allowance and gone through about half of the extra credits I bought. Most of my use is in Hermes, working on code, discussing technical decisions, and having the main agent delegate work to subagents.
The biggest improvement for me is how well it understands what I'm getting at.
I don't always start with a clear specification. Sometimes I'm talking through something that annoyed me, or using an analogy because I haven't quite worked out how to describe the problem. With Astra, I find myself spending less time saying, "No, that's not what I meant."
One example was a conversation about delegation. I compared the main agent to a manager assigning work. I wanted it to give the worker enough background and guidance, but leave the worker room to investigate and make decisions. If the main agent has already done the entire investigation and worked out the solution, handing off the last bit of implementation isn't saving much.
It understood that I wasn't asking it to give workers less context. I wanted it to stop doing their job before assigning it to them. That's the kind of distinction it seems better at picking up.
I've also liked its reasoning when discussing how the workflow should behave. I want it to keep working without asking me to approve every technical step, but I don't want it to quietly make product decisions for me. Those requests can sound contradictory if you turn them into blanket rules. Astra seems better at working out where the boundary belongs.
I still have frustrations with it, though. Sometimes it gives a good explanation or a progress update and then stops, even when there's more work it could do. I have to tell it to continue. This is especially annoying when we've just discussed why it should finish and verify the task without waiting for another prompt.
And when subagents are involved, waiting and rework can still make the whole thing slow. Being happier with the main model's reasoning doesn't mean I'm getting everything done faster.
I can't say yet whether it's worth the extra spend. I've been using it heavily, but the tasks and my Hermes setup have also been changing, so I don't have a clean comparison.
What I do notice is that talking through a messy problem with it takes less effort. That's a big part of what I use the main agent for, and it's the improvement I've felt most strongly. I still need to supervise the follow-through more than I'd like.
If you're using Astra in Hermes, has it reduced how often you need to correct or redirect the agent? Or are you mostly noticing better conversations, with the same amount of supervision afterward?
